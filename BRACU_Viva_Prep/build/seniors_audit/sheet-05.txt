B43: K-fold Cross Validation
D43: K-Fold Cross Validation is a model validation technique used to assess how well a machine learning model generalizes to unseen data. It helps to prevent overfitting and ensures that the model's performance is robust across different subsets of data.

The main idea is to split the dataset into K subsets (or "folds"), and then use each fold in turn as a testing set while training the model on the remaining K-1 folds. This process is repeated K times, and the model's performance is averaged over the K trials to get a final performance estimate.
F43: The evaluation score (accuracy, precision, etc.) that you get from each fold reflects how well the model is likely to perform on unseen data. The purpose is not to choose a "best model" from the K trained models but to estimate the generalization ability of the model. The final performance estimate is the average score across the K folds.
B45: Hypothesis
D45: a hypothesis refers to the model or function that the algorithm creates to map input data (features) to the output (predictions). It is essentially the candidate solution to the problem that the algorithm learns during the training process.
F45: The hypothesis should not just fit the training data well but also generalize to new, unseen data. A good hypothesis is one that captures the underlying patterns in the data and is not overly complex (which would lead to overfitting) or too simple (which could lead to underfitting).

B66: Ensemble Learning
D66: Ensemble learning is a technique in machine learning where you combine multiple models—often called “weak learners”—to create one strong model. The idea is that while each individual model might make mistakes, by combining their predictions you can reduce errors and improve overall performance.
F66: Diversity: Each model might see the data slightly differently or make different errors.
Combination: The final prediction is made by combining (averaging, voting, or using a meta-model) the outputs of individual models.
Improved Accuracy & Robustness: Ensembles tend to perform better and be more robust than a single model, as they can compensate for each other’s weaknesses.
B68: Boosting
D68: Boosting builds a strong model by training several weak models sequentially. Each model focuses more on the mistakes made by the previous ones.
B70: Bagging
D70: Bagging aims to reduce the variance of a model by training multiple models independently on different random samples of the data.
B72: Stacking
D73: Stacking combines multiple different models (which could be of various types) by training a “meta-model” that learns how best to combine their predictions.
